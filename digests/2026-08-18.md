# World News Digest — Week of August 11–18, 2026

## Conflict & Diplomacy

**Putin visits disputed Kuril island, angers Japan**
Russian President Vladimir Putin made the first-ever visit by a Russian leader to one of the Kuril islands disputed with Japan, drawing a swift, angry response from Tokyo over the long-running territorial dispute.

**Kushner meets Hamas in new Gaza push; more Iran sanctions**
Jared Kushner held a rare direct meeting with Hamas leadership in Egypt as part of a fresh push to revive the stalled Gaza ceasefire. Separately, the US imposed its eighth round of sanctions on Iran this year amid ongoing tension over the Iran conflict, which is also keeping oil prices elevated (Brent near $88.50/barrel).

**Libyan unification plan clouded by assassination**
Fawzi al-Mansouri, a figure tied to an LNA stronghold, was assassinated outside a mosque — a blow to the Haftar family's security standing that could delay a US-brokered plan to reunify Libya's rival governments.

**Russian opposition politician jailed for 11 years**
A Russian court convicted a prominent opposition politician for criticizing the war in Ukraine, sentencing him to 11 years and a month in prison, part of a broader crackdown on dissent ahead of next month's parliamentary election.

**Afghanistan marks five years of Taliban rule**
This past Saturday marked the fifth anniversary of the Taliban's return to power, with continued severe restrictions on girls' education highlighted as one of the starkest impacts.

## Disasters & Climate

**Typhoon Dolphin forces mass evacuation in China**
Typhoon Dolphin made landfall in China, triggering the evacuation of more than a million people and unleashing torrential rain that paralyzed transportation across affected regions.

**Deadly earthquake strikes western Colombia**
A quake hit western Colombia, with the towns of Zarzal, Roldanillo and El Cairo reporting widespread damage. The health system was overwhelmed, making the response one of Colombia's largest disaster operations in years.

**Europe's summer drought deepens**
Italy's Po River fell to historic lows during the country's fourth heatwave of the summer, straining water supplies and farming in the north. In England, three-quarters of the country is now in drought status, with 27 million people under water-use restrictions as a fifth heatwave approaches.

## Economy & Markets

**Fed rate-hike odds fade on soft US data**
Traders now price roughly a 30% chance of a September Fed move after weak US retail sales and consumer sentiment readings; Treasury yields eased slightly on the news.

**China's July data raises growth questions**
Newly released Chinese data pointed to industrial output growth of about 4.8% but a soft 1.5% rise in retail sales for July, fueling concern the world's second-largest economy is losing momentum.

**Japan's Q2 growth misses expectations**
Japan's economy grew 1.1% annualized in the second quarter, well short of the 2% forecast, as weak domestic demand offset strong export performance.

## Science & Space

**Total solar eclipse crosses Europe**
A total solar eclipse swept across parts of Europe on August 12, with Spain offering some of the best viewing — the event drew large public and scientific interest.

**"Black hole star" discovery**
Researchers identified a new class of object dubbed a "black hole star" — an early-universe, rapidly growing black hole wrapped in dense gas — offering a possible explanation for how billion-solar-mass black holes formed so soon after the Big Bang.

**Race to Mars' moons heats up**
Japanese scientists are preparing a sample-return mission to Mars' moons to probe the origins of the solar system and life on Earth, racing a rival Chinese mission aiming to get samples back first.
